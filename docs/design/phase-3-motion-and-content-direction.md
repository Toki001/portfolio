## Two materially different hero directions

**A — Portrait crossing the name.** A centered rectangular photograph overlaps full-width outlined identity, with a small introduction in the lower corner. It borrows the portrait/type tension of Matthew. Rejected for this source: the authorized square photo includes its own background, and overlap would conceal either the face or long name. A fabricated cutout/reconstructed face is prohibited.

**B — Open identity spread (selected).** An oversized two-line name sits in an open typographic grid, with the original portrait offset lower/right as an unframed rectangular photographic panel. Supporting role/copy/actions sit on their own baseline; the About link moves out of the photograph. A short burgundy rule supplies emphasis. The portrait enters from a side mask after the name begins. On phones, the name, concise positioning, photograph and actions stack in intentional reading order.

This removes the thick burgundy horseshoe, all curved photo masks, architectural grid, decorative arc/code glyph, circular outline/light and overlaid photo button. Straight image edges and open whitespace replace the door/window idea itself. Keep `portrait-stage` only as an implementation selector where useful; it must not retain the old visual. Do not edit facial appearance. Image failure leaves the reserved rectangle, identity, alt text and separate links readable.

Implement responsive line grouping from actual text geometry after fonts settle; preserve natural wrap on resize/zoom. No character-by-character effect. Stage offscreen content progressively, reveal promptly, cancel to final state on keyboard/reduction/hidden tab, and avoid replay on scroll-back. A fast scroller must never wait for a paragraph to finish. Heading order and real DOM text remain intact. One shared observer and finite compositor effects; no scroll-wrapper, prevented wheel, parent-variable animation storm, or continuous React position state.
